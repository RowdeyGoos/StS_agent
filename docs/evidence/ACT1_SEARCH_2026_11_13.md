# Searched combat in Act 1 — 2026-11-13

Status: **development result on fresh validation campaigns**. Search stays
opt-in: the registered gate is not cleared. The user approved the measurement
and chose its size (24 simulations, 512 campaigns) after a 16-campaign pilot.
A [follow-up](#follow-up-same-day-which-fights-end-the-campaigns) classifies
the fights that end the campaigns. A
[second one](#follow-up-same-day-the-campaign-policy-makes-the-noncombat-choices)
repeats the comparison with the campaign policy making the noncombat choices:
there the clear rate rises from 42.8% to 50.6%.

## Result

With 24-simulation search in combat, the same network clears Act 1 in **27.5%**
of 512 campaigns, against **18.2%** without search: **+9.4 points**
[+5.5, +13.3]. Search cleared 76 campaigns that the network alone lost, and
lost 28 that the network alone cleared.

| Measure, 512 paired campaigns | Network | Network with search | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cleared Act 1 | 93 (18.2%) | 141 (27.5%) | +9.4 [+5.5, +13.3] |
| Reached the boss | 328 (64.1%) | 379 (74.0%) | +10.0 [+6.1, +13.7] |
| Boss won, of the campaigns that reached it | 28.4% | 37.2% | not paired |
| Ended on floors 1–8 | 98 | 59 | |
| Ended on floors 9–15 | 86 | 74 | |
| Mean final floor | 13.60 | 14.45 | |
| Combats won | 3,398 of 3,817 | 3,657 of 4,028 | |

Brackets are 95% intervals from a bootstrap over campaigns. An exact sign test
on the 104 campaigns that only one arm cleared gives p = 0.000003.

- **The gain appears in both regions.** Overgrowth: 20.3% to 26.6%, +6.2
  [+1.5, +11.3]. Underdocks: 16.0% to 28.5%, +12.5 [+6.8, +18.1].
- **Search helps before the boss and at the boss.** It brings 51 more
  campaigns to the boss, mostly by surviving floors 1–8. In the 301 campaigns
  where both arms reached the boss, search won it 122 times and the network 88
  times.
- **The registered gate calls the result inconclusive.** The command's own
  conclusion uses the conservative Hoeffding interval, which is −2.6 to +21.4
  points here. Its radius is 12.0 points at 512 campaigns. At the same observed
  difference it would exclude zero from about 840 campaigns on.
- **Bosses are won less often here than on the benchmark.** The same network
  wins 48.7% of the benchmark's boss fights and 55.5% with search, against 28.4%
  and 37.2% of the bosses it reaches here. The benchmark's decks were built by
  the Act 1 campaign policy; here the noncombat heuristic builds them. The two
  panels differ in more than that, so this is an observation, not a measured
  effect of the decks. The follow-up finds that lower HP on arrival goes with
  a third to a half of the difference.

## What it means

Open question 3 of the [overview](../COMBAT_OVERVIEW.md#what-is-not-known) is
answered for 24 simulations: searched combat is worth about 9 Act 1 clear
points with the fixed noncombat heuristic. The earlier check had four paired
campaigns.

The result does not change a default. `sts-agent-evaluate` labels the report
`experimental_only`, and the [decision](../../DECISIONS.md) keeps search opt-in
until the paired Act 1 gate is cleared.

## Method and checks

- `sts-agent-evaluate --search --act1` plays every campaign twice from the
  same seed: once with the combat network alone and once with the same network
  under search. The noncombat heuristic makes every other choice in both arms.
- Settings are those of the search benchmark: `combat_belief_v1`, 24
  simulations, depth 16, four leaf steps, two belief particles, root baseline
  skipped. Checkpoint `ppo_v1:a6a0dac3…` (the benchmark's best network),
  noncombat policy `full_run_demo_v2:9c78a9e8…`.
- 512 validation-split campaigns from start index 77,000,000, alternating
  between the two regions. No earlier script or record names that index. The
  pilot used 16 campaigns from index 76,000,000 and is not part of the result.
- All 1,024 campaigns ran to their end: no cutoff, no failure, none
  unattempted.
- Search chose 53,339 of the 68,199 combat decisions in its arm (78.2%). The
  other 14,860 had one legal action. No decision fell back for any other
  reason, and no search or belief limit was hit.
- Search took 1.10 s per searched decision (1,280,136 simulations, 58,850
  worker-seconds), in line with 1.12 s on the benchmark's bosses after the
  [speed change](SEARCH_PROFILE_2026_11_13.md#follow-up-same-day-the-three-contained-changes).
- The analysis reproduces the command's own counts: 93 and 141 clears, 76 and
  28 one-sided clears.

## Limits

- One checkpoint, one search seed and one simulation count. Search at 96
  simulations was not run in Act 1.
- Fresh validation campaigns, not a held-out test. The source tree held
  uncommitted changes (build `188484ae…`, rules `4af09d99…`): the search speed
  change and two engine fixes.
- The two arms of a pair share a seed but diverge after their first different
  combat decision. They meet different fights, rewards and bosses afterwards,
  so a pair is the same start, not the same fights.
- "Reached the boss" means the campaign ended on floor 16. Boss rates given
  per arm compare different sets of campaigns.
- The report's combat summaries carry no encounter or room kind. The follow-up
  below reads them from the recorded trajectories.
- Clear rates depend on the noncombat heuristic. They cannot be compared with
  Act 1 results in which a campaign policy made those choices.

## Follow-up, same day: which fights end the campaigns

Status: **descriptive breakdown** of the same 1,024 recorded campaigns, with no
new runs. The user approved it after the result above.

With search, **the boss ends 64% of the lost campaigns** (238 of 371). Elites
end 22% and normal fights 14%.

| Where the 512 campaigns end | Network | Network with search |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cleared Act 1 | 93 | 141 |
| Lost at the boss | 235 | 238 |
| Lost at an elite | 90 | 81 |
| Lost in a normal fight | 94 | 52 |

| Fights won, by room | Network | Network with search |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Normal | 3,068 of 3,162 (97.0%) | 3,239 of 3,291 (98.4%) |
| Elite | 237 of 327 (72.5%) | 277 of 358 (77.4%) |
| Boss | 93 of 328 (28.4%) | 141 of 379 (37.2%) |

- **Search's gain has two parts.** 42 fewer campaigns die in normal fights and
  9 fewer at elites, so 51 more reach the boss. Boss losses stay level (235 and
  238) while boss wins rise by 48.
- **Search saves little HP in a fight both arms win.** The first fight of a
  pair is identical in both arms, and both won all 512. Search lost 5.8 HP in
  it against 6.6, 0.8 less [0.5, 1.1]; in 369 pairs the loss was equal.
  Search values a win at 1 plus a tenth of the HP fraction kept, which is the
  objective its critic was trained on. How much HP a player could keep in
  these fights is unmeasured.

**Bosses.** Every boss is won less often here than on the benchmark, the two
hardest by the least and Ceremonial Beast by the most.

| Boss | Network | Network with search | Benchmark: network, search |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Soul Fysh | 18 of 52 (35%) | 32 of 61 (52%) | 59.8%, 66.0% |
| Vantom | 21 of 58 (36%) | 27 of 61 (44%) | 55.0%, 63.3% |
| Ceremonial Beast | 17 of 49 (35%) | 22 of 59 (37%) | 78.0%, 82.0% |
| Lagavulin Matriarch | 13 of 62 (21%) | 26 of 77 (34%) | 28.2%, 35.3% |
| The Kin | 14 of 49 (29%) | 19 of 59 (32%) | 48.0%, 60.0% |
| Waterfall Giant | 10 of 58 (17%) | 15 of 62 (24%) | 23.0%, 26.4% |

**HP on arrival at the boss.** These campaigns arrive with less HP than the
benchmark's: 75% of maximum on average without search and 74% with it, against
84%. Six in ten of the benchmark's boss fights start with at least 85% HP; here
a quarter to three in ten do.

| HP on entry | Network | Network with search | Benchmark: share of fights, network, search |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Below 50% | 2 of 32 (6%) | 6 of 35 (17%) | 5.9%, 8.6%, 11.4% |
| 50% to 70% | 7 of 67 (10%) | 16 of 101 (16%) | 18.0%, 23.6%, 33.0% |
| 70% to 85% | 34 of 132 (26%) | 75 of 154 (49%) | 16.1%, 45.3%, 50.5% |
| 85% or more | 50 of 97 (52%) | 44 of 89 (49%) | 59.9%, 63.2%, 70.3% |

With search, bosses entered with at least 70% HP are won 49% of the time (119
of 243) and those entered below it 16% (22 of 136).

Applying the benchmark's win rate in each HP band to this run's arrivals gives
42.6% for the network and 46.9% with search, against 49.9% and 56.9% of the
benchmark's fights unweighted. HP on arrival is thus associated with about a
third of the gap for the network (7 of 21 points) and half of it with search
(10 of 20). The rest appears at equal HP and is not explained here. Decks,
relics and potions differ, and the network was trained on fights with the
campaign policy's decks, not the heuristic's. These were not separated.

**Elites.** Search loses fewer fights against Phantasmal Gardeners (18 of 62
against 24 of 54) and Skulking Colony (2 of 64 against 8 of 58). The other four
elites are level. Elite win rates are also below the benchmark's (77.4% against
85.8% with search).

**Method and limits.**
- A fight is the run of combat decisions on one floor of a recorded public
  trajectory. Its room is the map node the player stands on, its enemies are
  those shown at its first decision, and entry HP is the HP at that decision.
  The 7,845 fights found match the report's combat summaries one to one.
- The two arms are not matched fight samples after their first different
  decision. Only the first fight is paired. Per-boss and per-band counts are
  small: a rate from 60 fights is uncertain by about 12 points either way.
- The HP adjustment is a standardisation over four bands, not a causal
  estimate. It does not adjust for the mix of bosses.
- The comparison with the benchmark crosses panels. The benchmark weights each
  boss equally on its scoreboard; the figures used here are unweighted.

```bash
.venv/bin/python runs/act1-search-20261113/fights.py s24-n512 \
  runs/act1-search-20261113/fights-analysis.json
```

in `s24-n512/fights.json`. The benchmark's figures by HP band come from
`runs/combat-search96-20261113/analysis.json`. Four minutes passed between the
first extraction and the final tables. User wait: none.

## Follow-up, same day: the campaign policy makes the noncombat choices

Status: **development result on the same 512 validation starts**. The user
approved the rerun and lifted, for this one check, the roadmap's rule that
hybrid Act 1 checks keep the noncombat heuristic fixed. Search stays opt-in.

The first follow-up left a question: bosses are won far less often in these
campaigns than on the benchmark. The benchmark's fights come from campaigns of
the Act 1 500k campaign policy. Here that policy makes every noncombat choice
in both arms, and the combat network plays the fights as before.

| 512 starts, the same for all four players | Cleared Act 1 | Reached the boss | Boss won when reached |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Heuristic choices, network | 93 (18.2%) | 328 (64.1%) | 28.4% |
| Heuristic choices, network with search | 141 (27.5%) | 379 (74.0%) | 37.2% |
| Campaign policy's choices, network | 219 (42.8%) | 432 (84.4%) | 50.7% |
| Campaign policy's choices, network with search | 259 (50.6%) | 458 (89.5%) | 56.6% |

- **Search adds about as much as before.** With the campaign policy's choices
  it lifts the clear rate by +7.8 points [+3.9, +11.7]: 76 starts cleared only
  with search and 36 only without (sign test p = 0.0002). The gain with the
  heuristic was +9.4; the two differ by −1.6 [−7.2, +4.1].
- **Who makes the noncombat choices matters about three times as much.** On
  the same starts the campaign policy's choices add +24.6 points [+19.5,
  +29.5] for the network and +23.0 [+17.6, +28.5] for the network with search.
- **The benchmark's boss rates carry over.** The bosses reached here are won
  50.7% of the time by the network and 56.6% with search. The benchmark gives
  48.7% and 55.5% with equal weight per boss, 49.9% and 56.9% unweighted.
- **The registered gate still reads inconclusive.** Its Hoeffding interval for
  the gain from search is −4.2 to +19.8 points.
- **The boss ends 79% of the lost campaigns** with search (199 of 253). Elites
  end 25, normal fights 25 and four end outside a fight.

**Why the heuristic's campaigns do worse.** Not for lack of upgrades or
relics: they reach the boss with more of both. They arrive with less HP, die
more often at elites, and win fewer bosses at equal HP.

| Carried into the boss fight, means | Heuristic choices | Campaign policy's choices | Benchmark |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| HP on entry | 74% | 88% | 84% |
| Cards in the deck | 20.7 | 20.1 | 19.2 |
| Upgraded cards | 2.1 | 0.5 | 0.4 |
| Relics | 4.2 | 3.8 | 3.3 |
| Potions | 0.56 | 0.56 | 0.65 |

The figures are for the search arm; the network arm differs by at most 0.1,
and by two points in HP for the heuristic.

| Per campaign, search arm | Heuristic choices | Campaign policy's choices |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Normal fights | 6.4, 98.4% won | 8.1, 99.4% won |
| Elite fights | 0.70, 77.4% won | 0.51, 90.5% won |
| Campaigns lost at an elite | 81 | 25 |
| Campaigns lost in a normal fight | 52 | 25 |
| Bosses entered with at least 85% HP | 23%, of which 49% won | 67%, of which 70% won |

The campaign policy fights fewer elites, almost never upgrades and arrives
nearly healed. Its bosses at 85% HP or more are won as often as the
benchmark's (70.3%). Which of its choices matter most was not separated, and
neither was the network's familiarity with these decks: it was trained on
fights from this policy's campaigns.

**Bosses, search arm.**

| Boss | Campaign policy's choices | Heuristic choices | Benchmark |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Ceremonial Beast | 65 of 85 (76%) | 22 of 59 (37%) | 82.0% |
| Soul Fysh | 47 of 66 (71%) | 32 of 61 (52%) | 66.0% |
| Vantom | 55 of 82 (67%) | 27 of 61 (44%) | 63.3% |
| Lagavulin Matriarch | 49 of 81 (60%) | 26 of 77 (34%) | 35.3% |
| The Kin | 28 of 72 (39%) | 19 of 59 (32%) | 60.0% |
| Waterfall Giant | 15 of 72 (21%) | 15 of 62 (24%) | 26.4% |

Two bosses differ from the benchmark by more than these counts explain:
Lagavulin Matriarch is won more often here and The Kin less. The benchmark's
campaigns were not played the same way: the collector also played their
fights, and half of them were routed towards elites.

**What it means.**
- The heuristic check understated the combat agent. With the noncombat
  choices its fights were built on, the network with 24-simulation search
  clears Act 1 in about half of the campaigns.
- The boss and elite benchmark is a fair stand-in for campaign play with this
  campaign policy, and the boss is where four in five lost campaigns end.
- On these starts the noncombat choices are worth three times what search is.

**Method and checks.**
- `sts-agent-evaluate --search --act1` gained `--noncombat-checkpoint`. The
  named campaign bundle makes the noncombat choices of every arm with its
  greedy choice; without the option the heuristic decides as before. The
  report names the bundle as `noncombat_policy`, and the recorded trajectories
  carry a combined identity listed under `trajectory_policies`. Two new tests
  cover the option; 161 tests in the affected files pass.
- Campaign policy `ppo_v1:fce362d2…`, the benchmark's collector. Everything
  else is unchanged: the combat checkpoint, the search settings and the 512
  starts from index 77,000,000. Build `724c7180…`; the game rules are the same
  as in the heuristic run (`4af09d99…`).
- All four players of every start show the same first public decision, so
  the two runs are paired by start.
- All 1,024 campaigns ran to their end with no cutoff or failure. Search took
  1.11 s per searched decision.
- Search chose 55,416 of the 71,590 combat decisions in its arm. 15,483 had
  one legal action. In 691 (1.0%) search fell back to the network because the
  belief does not support the fight: 196 in fights started by events and 495
  after the Silver Crucible relic. The heuristic's campaigns had none.
- In the identical first fight search again lost 0.8 HP less than the network
  (6.0 against 6.8).

**Limits.**
- One campaign policy, one combat checkpoint, one search seed, 24 simulations.
- The campaign policy playing its own fights was not run on these starts, so
  the gain from replacing its combat play is not measured here.
- The comparison with the benchmark crosses panels, and per-boss counts are
  small: a rate from 75 fights is uncertain by about 11 points either way.
- The pilot used the earlier pilot's 16 starts (network 6 clears, search 8)
  and is not part of the result.
